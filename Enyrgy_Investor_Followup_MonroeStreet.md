## Notes

**Short on purpose.** He heard the story this morning in Scott's voice. Retelling it in writing is weaker than the memory of it, and repeating metrics he just heard reads as though Scott were not sure he was listening. What he needs is a route through the room, since he was told there is a lot in it.

**The listing vehicle paragraph offers rather than asks.** An earlier draft read "that is the conversation I am most interested in", which put Scott's outcome at the center of a relationship that is one call old, and "on your side" framed it as two parties across a table. It now offers to arm him for his own conversation and invites a discouraging answer, which costs nothing and signals that the read matters more than the yes.

**The clinical claims from the call are not restated in writing.** They belong in the data room with their own framing rather than in a forwardable email from the CEO. Nothing was cut that he has not already heard.
