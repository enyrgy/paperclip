**Body:**

```
A useful thing to understand, whether or not you ever buy anything from us.

Light does different work at different times of day. Morning light, especially, sets your circadian clock. It tells your body when to be alert and, twelve to fourteen hours later, when to release melatonin for sleep. This is why a few minutes of morning light outdoors improves a lot of people's sleep more than any supplement they have tried.

Midday light does something different. That is when the sun is high enough for UVB to actually reach the angle that drives vitamin D synthesis. The practical takeaway costs nothing: get some light early for your clock, and understand that the synthesis pathway specifically needs the midday UVB window, which most modern schedules and most latitudes do not reliably provide for much of the year. That gap is the whole reason controlled UVB exists.

No ask today. Just something worth knowing.
```

The Session 14 audit recorded this and declared it decoupled everywhere:

> **Guarantee vs lab-timeline contradiction:** several emails tied the 30-day money-back window to an 8-to-12-week lab retest ("risk only a lab fee"), which is impossible inside 30 days. Decoupled into two separate beats everywhere: labs are your own long-term proof, the 30-day guarantee is an independent keep-or-return decision.

**Touch 7 still carried it**, almost in the flagged words: *"Retest at eight to twelve weeks... The 30-day money-back guarantee exists so the only thing you genuinely risk is the cost of a lab test."* A reader following that instruction returns at week ten to find the window closed six weeks earlier.

**Touch 4 carried it more compactly:** *"a 30-day money-back guarantee built precisely so you can test it against your own labs. Baseline before, retest after, decide on data."*

WF-17 looks like where the error originally lived. The fix either never landed here or was reverted.

Both were found only by extracting the copy and reading it, which is the argument for finishing this backup rather than sampling it.
