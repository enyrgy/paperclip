# If they raise a health condition, a medication, or an injury

**This will happen.** It is the most likely turn a call about being tired takes, and it is the one place a wrong answer causes real harm.

**Never tell anyone they are fine.** Not "that should be OK", not "I don't think that's on the list", not "plenty of people with that use it." Absence from the list is not clearance and you have no standing to give it.

**Say this:**

> I'm not the right person to answer that, and I don't want to guess about your health. There is a contraindication list that ships with every unit, and I can send it to you today so you have it in front of you. The question of whether it is right for you is one for your doctor, and they can answer it in a few minutes.

Then send the list, and **flag the contact to Scott the same day.** Do not run that conversation yourself.

**Never answer a health question with the product story.** Someone describing a condition is asking whether this helps or harms them. Listing the three pathways answers a different question and sounds like a yes.

**Never write health details into GHL.** Not in a note, not a custom field, not a tag. If they volunteer something, escalate the conversation rather than recording it.

# Words that never come out of your mouth

treat, cure, diagnose, disease, prescription, FDA-approved, medical treatment, heal, fix, medication, illness, clinical diagnosis, therapeutic treatment.

Enyrgy is a wellness device and makes no medical claims. It raises and documents Vitamin D levels. That is the whole claim, and it is a strong one.

**Two more, specific to this call:**

- Say **users**, not customers. A home unit supports up to six people.
- If the 12-week study comes up: **five participants, no control group, and we say so.** Never present it as more than it is. "Small study, real numbers" holds up better than a bigger claim would.

# Two notes on why the call is built this way

**The offer comes fifth, not first.** Opening with a discount tells the prospect the product needed one. These people have had five educational emails and no pitch, so the call has permission to be a conversation. Asking whether it landed, and whether they have ever had a level tested, does two things a price-first opening cannot: it finds out whether they are a real buyer, and it makes the offer land as a reason to act now rather than the reason to be interested at all.

**The thing most likely to go wrong is not the pitch.** It is that someone says "I have lupus, would this help me?" and the natural instinct is to be helpful. Lupus erythematosus is on the contraindication list. The scripted answer above exists so that instinct has somewhere to go that is not an answer.
