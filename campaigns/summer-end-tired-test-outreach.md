# THE PHONE SCRIPT

## Opening

> Hi, is this {{first_name}}? This is Millie from Enyrgy. You took our Tired Test a little while back and have been getting the emails from Scott. Is now an OK time for two minutes?

**If no:** ask when is better, book it, end the call. Do not pitch into a no.

## Get them talking before you say anything about price

> The reason I'm calling rather than emailing is simple. Most people who take that test recognize themselves in it, and then life happens and it sits. I wanted to ask directly: did any of it land for you?

Then actually listen. Two follow-ups worth having ready:

> Have you ever had your Vitamin D level tested?

> Are you taking a supplement now?

**Why these two.** If they have a number, that is the whole conversation, because the platform's job is to move a number. If they take a supplement and feel no different, that is the one-in-four low-responder point they already read about, and now it is about them rather than a statistic.

## The bridge

> Here is where it fits. Supplements are one lane, and in research clinical trials about one in four participants were low responders, so a capsule does not reliably move the number for everyone. The platform runs three pathways in a two to four minute session, and the app sets the dose from your skin type and shuts it off on its own. The point is that you retest in twelve weeks and see what actually happened.

## The offer

> The reason for the timing. We are running a Summer End offer: $500 off and free shipping, through midnight on August 31. That takes it from $2,995 to $2,495. After the 31st it goes back.

Then stop talking. Let them react.

## If they raise a health condition, a medication, or an injury

**This will happen.** It is the most likely thing to come up on a call about being tired, and it is the one place a wrong answer causes real harm.

**Never tell anyone they are fine.** Not "that should be OK", not "I don't think that's on the list", not "plenty of people with that use it." Absence from the list is not clearance and you have no standing to give it.

**Say this:**

> I'm not the right person to answer that, and I don't want to guess about your health. There is a contraindication list that ships with every unit, and I can send it to you today so you have it in front of you. The question of whether it is right for you is one for your doctor, and they can answer it in a few minutes.

Then send the list, and **flag the contact to Scott the same day.** Do not run that conversation yourself.

**Never answer a health question with the product story.** Someone describing a condition is asking whether this helps or harms them. Listing the three pathways answers a different question and sounds like a yes.

**Never write health details into GHL.** Not in a note, not in a custom field, not in a tag. If they volunteer something, escalate the conversation instead of recording it.

## Words that never come out of your mouth

treat, cure, diagnose, disease, prescription, FDA-approved, medical treatment, heal, fix, medication, illness, clinical diagnosis, therapeutic treatment.

Enyrgy is a wellness device and makes no medical claims. It raises and documents Vitamin D levels. That is the whole claim, and it is a strong one.

**Two more, specific to this call:**

- Say **users**, not customers. A home unit supports up to six people.
- If the 12 week study comes up: **five participants, no control group, and we say so.** Never present it as more than it is. "Small study, real numbers" is the honest framing and it holds up better than a bigger claim would.

## Why the offer sits fifth in the call, not first

A discount opening tells the prospect the product needed a discount. These leads have had five educational emails and no pitch, so the call has permission to be a conversation. Asking whether any of it landed, and whether they have ever had a level tested, does two things a price-first opening cannot: it finds out whether they are a real buyer, and it makes the offer land as a reason to act now rather than as the reason to be interested at all.

## The one that will actually go wrong

Not the pitch. A prospect will say something like "I have lupus, would this help me?" and the natural human instinct is to be helpful. Lupus erythematosus is on the contraindication list. The scripted answer exists so that instinct has somewhere to go that is not an answer.
