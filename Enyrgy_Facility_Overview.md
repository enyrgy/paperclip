## WHAT WE MEASURED, AND WHAT IT DOES NOT SHOW

Twelve weeks. Five healthy adults, skin types II to IV, four to six sessions a week. No control group.

**This is our own study, not independent research, and five people is a small study.** We would rather hand you every row than an average.

| Participant | Skin type | Start, ng/mL | End, ng/mL |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | II | 38.4 | 86.0 |
| P2 | III | 34.0 | 88.4 |
| P3 | III | 37.0 | 78.0 |
| P4 | IV | 47.0 | 88.7 |
| P5 | III | 43.4 | 79.9 |
| **Average** | | **39.96** | **84.20** |

A 111 percent average increase. Every participant started insufficient or borderline, every one reached optimal, and nobody dropped out.

What it shows is narrow and real: in a small group, consistent sessions moved the number, and moved it in all five. What it does not show is a controlled effect size, and we are not going to claim one from five people.

The study that will matter to you is the one you run in your own book, on your own patients, with your own lab. That is the point of the modality. It produces a number your team can watch.
